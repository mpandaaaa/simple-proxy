CHEMISTRY

THE MATHS OF CHEMISTRY

Links between club-mates and era-mates are worth up to +3 OVR every match. Here is the exact formula and the strategy it creates.

Written by the Perfect XI developer · Updated 8 July 2026

How links work

Chemistry counts links between the players you've placed. Two players link if they share a club or share a season. Each player counts a maximum of three links, and your chemistry percentage is total links divided by the maximum possible (three per player). Eleven players from eleven different clubs and seasons: 0%. A core built around one great side: the percentage climbs fast.

What it's worth

Chemistry converts to an on-pitch bonus of up to +3.0 effective OVR, applied before match strength is computed — 33% chemistry is worth about +1, 66% about +2, and a full-links squad the whole +3. That is the difference between an 86 squad and an 89 squad, which in odds terms is roughly one full expectation band. It is the single biggest free upgrade in the game.

The real decision it creates

The classic draft dilemma: the wheel offers a 91-rated superstar from a club you own nobody from, or an 87 who links twice into your existing spine. The 87 is frequently correct — two links move the whole squad, while the 91 moves one slot. The exception is the true outliers (95+); a Ronaldo or Henry spin is worth breaking chemistry for.

Era-locked drafts stack chemistry naturally — fewer clubs, shared seasons — which is why a locked era XI often outperforms a higher-rated mixed side. Dynasty transfers should respect links too: signing into an existing club core protects the bonus for seasons.

Reading the meter

The chemistry line under your draft overall updates live with every pick and shows the exact match-day bonus. If the number stalls, look for the player whose links haven't materialised and plan their upgrade in the next reroll or, in Dynasty, the next window.

A worked example

Take a spine of five players from Arsenal's 2003 squad plus six unlinked picks. Each Arsenal player links with the other four, capped at three links apiece: 5 × 3 = 15 links. Maximum possible is 3 per player × 11 = 33, so chemistry reads 45% — worth about +1.4 OVR in every match. Swap one unlinked pick for a sixth 2003 Gunner and you jump to 55% and +1.6; the marginal pick was "worth" roughly two rating points more than his card shows.

Common chemistry mistakes

Three patterns cost players the bonus. Chasing rating in isolation — a lone 90 in a linked squad often lowers your effective strength versus the linked 87. Splitting your core — two half-cores of one club each (say four United, four Liverpool) links fine, but a 3–3–5 spread across three clubs caps everyone early; consolidate where the wheel allows. Forgetting seasons link too — two players from different clubs in the same season still link, which is how mixed-club era squads keep respectable percentages.

Chemistry in practice: worked examples

Chemistry rewards links between players who genuinely belonged together. Two players from the same club and the same season form the strongest link; same club across different seasons is worth less; same season at different clubs is worth least. The total is capped at a +3 boost to your effective squad rating.

Is chemistry worth sacrificing rating for?

The honest answer is usually no, but the margin is narrower than people assume. A +3 chemistry swing is worth roughly the same as three points of squad average, which over 38 games is worth several league positions. If two available players are within two rating points of each other, take the one that links.

Where chemistry genuinely wins is in the middle of a draft, when the wheel keeps returning to clubs you have already drafted from. A second and third Arsenal 2003/04 player compound quickly.

The trap

The common mistake is chasing a full-chemistry XI from a single club-season. Almost no squad in the database has eleven players good enough across every position, so you end up with a brilliant spine and a weak defence. Chemistry is a tie-breaker, not a strategy.
