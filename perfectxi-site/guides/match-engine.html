THE ENGINE

HOW THE MATCH ENGINE WORKS

Your season is not a dice roll. Here is the pipeline from eleven player ratings to a full-time verdict — and what you control at each step.

Written by the Perfect XI developer · Updated 8 July 2026

From eleven ratings to one number

When your squad is complete, the engine reduces it to a single underlying strength built from your squad average. Formation applies attacking and defensive multipliers on top — a 4-3-3 tilts attack, a 5-4-1 tilts defence — and squad chemistry adds up to +3.0 to the effective average before strength is computed. That effective number drives everything: pre-season odds, match simulation, and the plausibility of a title push.

What the pre-season odds are telling you

The odds card on the squad screen is not decoration — it is the engine's honest read of your effective average against the league. As a rule of thumb from the tuning: squads averaging in the low 80s profile as top-half sides, mid-80s as European contenders, and you need high-80s effective strength before "title favourites" is realistic. If the odds look wrong to you, check chemistry first — a +2 link bonus moves you a full band.

Inside a simulated match

Each of the 38 fixtures is simulated event-by-event: goals are generated minute-by-minute with scorers and assists drawn from your actual XI, weighted by position and rating — which is why your Golden Boot race in a Dynasty season almost always features your forwards, but a marauding 90-rated midfielder can steal it. Results feed a running form strip, and clean sheets, biggest win and goal difference are tracked for the final review.

Difficulty, rerolls and variance

Difficulty changes your draft resources, not the match maths: easy gives three rerolls, normal one, hard none. The simulation itself is honestly random within its weightings — an 88-average squad will usually finish top four, sometimes win the league, and occasionally have a nightmare. Across many runs the averages hold; in any single season, variance is the drama. The 38-0 guide covers how far preparation can bend that variance.

The final verdict

Full-time, the engine grades the season — points, finish position, and the headline verdict (an unbeaten 38 earns INVINCIBLE). In Dynasty, that finish is also measured against the board's objective, which is where manager rating takes over. Every number on the results screen comes from the same pipeline described here: eleven ratings in, one season out.

A worked expectation

Concrete numbers help. An 88 effective average (say an 86.5 squad with strong chemistry) typically lands in the 78–92 point band: most seasons finish top four, a good share win the league, and roughly one run in ten goes sideways with 60-something points. Drop to an 82 effective average and the centre of gravity moves to 55–70 points — European places on a good day. The engine is honest about this before kick-off: the odds card is those distributions, summarised.

What you control — and what you don't

You control the inputs: eleven ratings, the formation multipliers, and the chemistry bonus. You do not control the dice inside a single match — and that is deliberate. A deterministic sim would make every 88-average season identical and killing the replay value; honest variance is why an unbeaten 38 is an achievement rather than a checkbox, and why a mid-table squad occasionally produces a season you screenshot.

Inside the numbers: what the engine actually computes

Every fixture starts by turning two teams into two expected-goal figures. Your XI’s effective strength — squad average, adjusted for formation and chemistry — becomes an attack parameter and a defence parameter on a logarithmic scale. Your opponent gets the same treatment. Expected goals for the home side are then the exponential of a base rate plus the home attack, plus the away defence, plus a home-advantage term.

That home-advantage term is 0.27 on the log scale, which works out at roughly a 1.31× multiplier on home scoring. It is not invented: it is the figure Dixon and Coles fitted to English league football, and it has held up remarkably well across three decades of data.

Why pure randomness gets football wrong

If you simply draw goals from two independent Poisson distributions, you systematically produce too few 0-0 and 1-1 draws and too many 1-0 wins. Real football has more low-scoring stalemates than independence predicts, because teams protect a point once a game gets late and level.

The engine corrects this with a factor applied to the four lowest scorelines, controlled by a parameter of −0.13. In practice, a 0-0 in an evenly matched fixture rises from roughly 8.2% to 9.9%, a 1-1 from 12.7% to 14.4%, and a 1-0 falls from 11.1% to 9.4%.

Tuned against real seasons

The parameters were calibrated until simulated output matched the shape of real Premier League seasons: about 2.75 goals per game, champions on roughly 90 points, mid-table around 52, and relegation near 30. An all-time XI can push past 100 points, which is right — the real record is 100.

The engine also tracks expected goals across your season, so you can see whether you finished above or below what your chances deserved. Beating your xG by ten goals means you got lucky, and it will probably regress next season in Dynasty.
