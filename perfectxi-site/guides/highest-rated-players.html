The surprises worth knowing

Some rows will start arguments, and a few are pure trivia gold. Peter Schmeichel is Aston Villa's highest-rated player — his 2001 stint outranks every outfield Villan in the database. Peter Beardsley at Bolton (88, 1997) and Sandro Tonali at Burnley (86, 2023) are the kind of late-career and loan anomalies the season-by-season format surfaces that a normal all-time list never would.

At the very top, only two clubs own a 95+: Manchester United with Ronaldo's 99 and Arsenal with Henry's 97 — the two most valuable single spins in the game. Erling Haaland's 95 makes Manchester City the only other club within touching distance.

Why "highest-rated" is club-specific

Ratings in Perfect XI are attached to a player-in-a-shirt, not just a player — a design choice explained in the ratings database guide. So Shearer shows twice in the 94 club: once for Blackburn (1992) and once for Newcastle. When you spin a club, you are drafting from that club's own history at those specific strengths.

Strategically, this table doubles as a spin-value chart. If the wheel offers a reroll and lands on a club whose ceiling is 80, spending it is usually right; landing on one of the 94+ clubs, take the room. More on reroll economy in the 38-0 guide.

Small clubs, real ceilings

The table also shows why underdog drafts are playable at all: even Barnsley's single Premier League season yields a 76 (Neil Redfearn), and most one-season clubs top out in the high 70s to low 80s. In weekly Moneyball events, where nobody above 85 is allowed, these entries stop being trivia and become picks.
